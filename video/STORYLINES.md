# Milo demo film — storylines (v2: X-style launch film + 3D route planning)

**The one thing:** Milo lets someone who can't easily type, click or speak get real things done — and it plans around their body, their chair and their world.
**One viewer, one task:** Maria (fictional) needs a power wheelchair approved and needs to get to a clinic safely.
**One piece of data travels through it:** her measurements — *hips 17″ · door 28″ · pushes ~20 ft* — become the fit check, the appeal evidence, and the route.

Format: 2:30–3:00, 16:9 1920×1080 @ 30 fps, launch-film pacing (fast cuts, kinetic type, big product close-ups like startup films on X), soft voiceover (ElevenLabs "Sarah"), burned-in captions, music bed + the app's real chimes.

**Honesty line for the 3D sequence:** the 3D city is an illustration of what Milo checks. Every check shown on it must be one Milo really does — accessible entrance (Google Places), rolling distance vs her push limit, least-walking transit, chair width vs doorways. Kerb cuts / slopes / broken lifts are shown as "call ahead" (Google doesn't provide them), never as solved. Label: "Illustration · demo data".

## A. "Three moves" — one day, one person · one continuous take ⭐ recommended

Why: a stranger gets it in 2 seconds (a real face, three head moves, a sentence spoken), and the 3D route is the payoff a judge remembers.

- **First 2 s:** her real webcam face, the orange nose dot on the grid; **I** lights up as her head tilts. No logo.
- **Beats (≈ seconds):**
  1. **Hook (0–12)** — I·W·W → "I want water", spoken in Lauren's voice, 64 px type. "3 moves · saved 9 letters".
  2. **Tension (12–25)** — the denial letter slams in: "not medically necessary". Kinetic type: *"Most AI agents assume you can type."*
  3. **Reveal (25–32)** — Milo hops in; name lands.
  4. **Proof (32–100)** — fast verbs, each a real UI close-up at 1.6–2×: *ask* (W·W·A → deadline) · *check* (2 ✅ / 7 ❌, Terra 27″+2″ > 28″ door) · *draft* (appeal builds) · *challenge* (strict reviewer, 2 objections → v2) · *verify & nod* (✓ → filed, confetti).
  5. **3D payoff (100–140)** — the camera lifts out of the app into a low-poly 3D Oakland. Her chair (to scale, 24.5″) rolls out of her home; Milo's route draws in orange: step-free station (✅), rolling segment counter vs her ~6 m push limit, a door frame check (26.5″ ≤ 28″ ✅), destination entrance badge from Google (✅ accessible entrance, ✅ restroom). A rejected route greys out with "stairs · call ahead".
  6. **Receipts (140–155)** — every step as a receipt row: mode, confidence, evidence, live sponsors.
  7. **Claim + end card (155–170)** — *"Works with whatever you can do."* Milo carries the wordmark in.
- **Look:** the app's world (cream, white cards, burnt orange, Lexend) → 3D city in the same palette (cream ground, white blocks, orange route, green ✅).
- **Sound:** soft lo-fi bed, the real chimes on every selection, calm voiceover; one bigger chord when the route lands.

## B. "Four ways in" — split screen to one · ends in 3D

- **First 2 s:** four panes: hands typing, a voice waveform, a nose dot, a hand fingerspelling — all asking "Where's my…".
- **Beats:** 4 inputs → collapse into one answer → denial → fit/appeal fast proof → the four panes fold into the 3D city where the route plans → end card.
- **Look:** each pane tinted by its mode, merging into one cream world.
- **Sound:** four soft voices resolving into one chord.

## C. "Map of a life" — the 3D world IS the film · object-to-object chain (bold)

- **First 2 s:** a 3D model of Maria's 28″ bathroom door; a chair rolls up and stops — "27″ + 2″ = 29″" glows red.
- **Beats:** the door → her home in 3D → the camera pulls up to the city → every task is a stop on the map: the clinic (appeal), the shop (checkout with Alex's approval), the library (accessible route) → the whole city lights orange → end card.
- **Look:** low-poly city, the app's palette, UI cards pinned to buildings.
- **Sound:** soft ambient city + chimes.
